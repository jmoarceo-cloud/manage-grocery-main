# HCI GroceryOne — 3-Minute Promotional Video
## InVideo AI Script, Visual Direction, and Production Notes

**Format:** 3 minutes · 16:9 landscape · English voice-over · accurate captions  
**Style:** high-end corporate technology launch, cinematic grocery footage, sleek product UI, and restrained dark premium transitions  
**Primary audience:** grocery owners, managers, and operations teams  
**Also relevant to:** shoppers, applicants, cashier and inventory staff, Finance, HR, supervisors, and employees

This is a promotional treatment of the checked-in HCI GroceryOne system—not a promise that every module is configured in production or that the system makes business decisions without people. It follows the four-part arc in the provided recommendation, but revises the claims and visuals to match the actual source.

---

## 1. The true product story

HCI GroceryOne connects public product discovery with role-based grocery operations and workforce workflows. Visitors can browse the public catalog; authorized staff use POS and HRMS entry points for their respective work. Completed POS sales update stock records. Inventory and purchasing follow review and approval paths. HR workflows cover applicants, employee records, attendance, requests, and payroll. AI can assist with product-information intake, but drafts require human review.

**Core line:** *One store. Connected work.*

**Story arc**

1. **0:00–0:30 — The hook:** A grocery day links many moving parts; introduce the connected-work idea without promising that software eliminates every challenge.
2. **0:30–1:15 — POS and visibility:** Show a real authorized checkout and how completed sales connect to stock and available reporting.
3. **1:15–2:00 — People and payroll:** Show role-appropriate HR/employee workflows and clearly explain payroll responsibilities.
4. **2:00–2:45 — Product information, inventory, and procurement:** Show AI as draft assistance, then human-reviewed replenishment, purchase approval, and receiving.
5. **2:45–3:00 — Call to action:** Invite viewers to browse the catalog or use authorized staff access. Do not promise a demo booking path that is not confirmed in the application.

## 4. Three-minute script and visual cues

Timing is approximate and totals exactly 3:00. The voice-over is written for a confident, energetic read with room to view the interface. Captions should match the final recorded narration exactly.

### [0:00–0:30] The hook — a connected grocery day

**Voice-over**

> Running a grocery store means keeping many things moving: products on the shelf, customers at checkout, deliveries on the way, and a team ready for the day. When information sits in separate places, it can be harder to follow what needs attention.  
>   
> HCI GroceryOne brings key grocery and workforce workflows into one connected system. One store. Connected work.

**Visual**

- Open with cinematic close-ups: a hand straightening products, a cashier preparing the counter, a delivery being checked, and a manager reviewing the day's work.
- Keep the store welcoming and realistic. Avoid acting that implies panic, disaster, or a measured business problem.
- A subtle blue line links shelf → checkout → stock room → team workspace.
- Move to the real landing page. Show its utility strip, sticky navigation, split hero, and catalog preview. Hold long enough to read **“One store. Connected work.”**
- Briefly show the supplied public-catalog screenshot as a distinct browse page: store-information chips, category sidebar, search, and grocery product cards. Keep it separate from the POS/cart experience.
- Transition between chapters with a brief deep-navy panel, restrained blue linework, and a simple chapter title; return to the actual light UI for screen content.

**On-screen text**

- **A grocery day has a lot in motion**
- **HCI GroceryOne**
- **One store. Connected work.**

**Accuracy note:** Do not say that every grocery operation uses paper or that this system automatically resolves all operational challenges.

## 5. Editing and production direction

### Visual rhythm

- Use cinematic grocery footage for context and human moments; use actual application captures for product truth.
- Dark premium backgrounds are chapter transitions, not a replacement for the real light landing-page design.
- Keep motion graphics simple: blue connection lines, restrained status transitions, and clean typography.
- Prefer a few deliberate cuts, gentle push-ins, and match cuts over constant transitions.
- Do not overpack the screen with labels. Show one main idea per shot.

### Audio

- Voice: warm, clear, confident, energetic, professional—not exaggerated or overly dramatic.
- Music: polished electronic pulse with warm piano/synth texture; gradually build, then resolve at the close.
- Mix the music beneath narration. Use quiet scan, tap, or transition sounds only when the real footage supports them.
- Check pronunciation and caption spelling for **HCI GroceryOne**, **POS**, **HRMS**, **QR**, and **Finance/Admin**.

### Accessibility and export

- 16:9 landscape, 1920×1080 or higher; keep essential text within a central safe area.
- Add synchronized English captions and manually verify them.
- Use legible, high-contrast titles with enough time to read; avoid placing captions over dense UI.
- Export a clean master. Keep captions and any localization as separate editable tracks where possible.

## 7. Claims to avoid

Do not include the following statements or visuals unless they are independently verified and genuinely supported by the live deployment:

- Public customer ordering, cart, or payment—the public catalog is for browsing; sales use authenticated POS.
- “Instant” or guaranteed analytics, inventory accuracy, profit-margin visibility, revenue growth, or faster checkout.
- Automated purchase orders, automatic supplier routing, automatic approval, or AI-driven purchasing decisions.
- One-click payroll, guaranteed on-time payment, error-free calculations, or guaranteed compliance.
- “Never lose a sale,” “perfectly stocked,” “zero manual work,” or “get your time back.”
- One shared POS/HRMS login, identical access for every employee, or unrestricted role access.
- AI-verified product or nutrition facts. Product AI output is draft information requiring human review.
- GPS/geofence as proof of identity or exact presence.
- Production readiness, 24/7 availability, measured savings, testimonials, or security guarantees not established by evidence.
- Real personal, employee, applicant, customer, credential, or confidential business data.
- “Book a demo today” unless a real, supported demo-booking path is added and verified.
